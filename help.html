    The manual
    MEGAjam

    Everything in the studio, explained in writing, with step-by-step walkthroughs. Read it front to back, or jump to the part you need from the contents. Prefer to learn by doing? The guided tours under Help in the studio walk you through the same ground with the app lighting the way.

    Chapter 1Welcome to MEGAjam

    MEGAjam is a shared music studio in your web browser. You and up to three friends walk into the same room from wherever you are, pick instruments, and play together, live.

    How it works, in one minute

    Most ways of playing music over the internet send sound from one place to another. Sound is heavy and arrives late, so the band never quite lines up.

    MEGAjam sends something much lighter: what your hands do. When you press a key, your browser sends a tiny message saying "this note, this hard, at this moment". Every browser in the room runs every player's instruments, so each one plays your note itself, at that exact moment, on a clock the whole room shares. Everyone hears the same music at the same instant.

    Think of it like a choir singing from the same score with the same conductor, instead of passing a microphone around. The notes are planned a tiny bit in the future (a few dozen milliseconds, the lookahead) so they always arrive in time.

    What you need

    • A browser. Chrome or Edge on a computer works best. Safari and Firefox work too, with a few limits (MIDI keyboards need Chrome, Edge or Firefox).
    • Headphones. They keep your speakers out of your microphone when you talk to the band, and they make timing feel tighter.
    • Optionally: a MIDI keyboard or controller, real synths, a microphone or guitar. None are required: your computer keyboard plays notes.

    Two rooms

    Every studio has two rooms, and you can walk between them any time from the top bar:

    • The Jam Room is for playing together live.
    • The Compose Room is for building a song: sections, chords and parts you can play back, edit and export.

    Talk and chat follow you between rooms, and your bandmates stay in the same studio wherever they are.

    Chapter 2Getting started

    Walkthrough: start your first jam
    1. Open MEGAjam. Type your name in Your name. It's what the band sees.
    2. Click Start a jam. A studio opens with a four-letter room code at the top.
    3. The first time, you'll see two doors. Pick Jam Room.
    4. The first time, MEGAjam also offers a first instrument: something to play keys on, something to strum, a bass, or drums. Pick one.
    5. Press A S D F on your computer keyboard. You're playing.
    Walkthrough: join a friend's jam
    1. Get the room's four-letter code or link from your friend.
    2. If you got a link, just open it. If you got a code, type your name, type the code in CODE, and click Walk in.
    3. You arrive in the same studio, in a corner of your own.

    Just listening

    Click or just listen in in the lobby (or open a listener link) to hear the band without playing. Your browser still plays every instrument itself, so it sounds exactly like being in the room. Listeners don't take a player's corner, and the band sees how many people are listening.

    "Tap to hear the room"

    Browsers keep sound off until you touch the page. If you open a link in a new tab or reload, MEGAjam may ask you to tap once. Do, and you'll hear a little chime when sound is on.

    New to this?

    New to this? Take the tour in the lobby starts a guided tour of your first jam. In the studio, Help (top right) lists all three, and this manual:

    • Your first jam: an instrument, a key, chord pads, a drummer, the tempo, recording, inviting friends.
    • Write your first song: chords, a drum beat, looping a section, a bass part, locking it in, playing the song.
    • Jam with a friend: a pretend bandmate called Robin joins you and shows how playing together works: the shared clock, chat and talk, hardware twins, matching sounds.

    Each step lights up one thing and waits until you've done it. Show me does it for you, and × leaves the tour at any step.

    The corners

    Each player has a corner along the wall: yours is lit in your colour, and empty ones wait for friends. A corner shows the player's name, their instruments, a light for how well their connection is doing, and a volume slider and M (mute) that set how loud that player is for you (nobody else's mix changes). Virtual drummers get corners of their own.

    Your desk

    On the left, Your gear is your shelf: the instruments in your corner, plus My gear (real synths) and Live audio. In the middle sits the selected instrument's faceplate, with its knobs (Chapter 4).

    The keys

    The on-screen keyboard plays the selected instrument. Above it: Rhythm help, Lock to key (once a key is set), Enable MIDI and Panic (Chapter 5).

    The side tabs

    Down the right edge: band chat, live audio, MIDI, and (technical) the wiring view. At the foot, the gear button shows or hides the technical controls: quantize, lookahead, per-instrument timing and MIDI channels, external MIDI and the wiring panel. MEGAjam starts simple; this browser remembers your choice.

    Chapter 4Instruments

    Instruments come from the shared studio's wall. Whatever you pick, the whole band hears exactly the same thing, because every browser runs the same instrument with the same settings.

    Walkthrough: put an instrument on your desk
    1. On your shelf, click the empty slot (+ Pick one or + Add). The wall of instruments opens.
    2. Click ▶ Hear it on any tile to audition it.
    3. Click a tile to take it. It goes on your desk, selected, and the keys play it.
    4. To play another, add it the same way, then click it on your shelf to select it.

    A desk holds up to 12 instruments (that many is mostly for sequencer rigs, Chapter 14). Remove, at the foot of the faceplate, takes the selected one off.

    The faceplate

    • Instrument and Patch (or Kit, for drums): swap the instrument or pick one of its starting sounds.
    • Knob pages: instruments with many knobs group them in pages (Filter, Envelope, and so on). Click a page name.
    • Knobs: drag up and down, scroll, or click one and use the arrow keys. Hold Shift for fine moves. Double-click puts a knob back to the patch's setting.
    • Audition plays a short phrase so you can hear your changes without playing.

    Every knob move travels to the band like a note, so everyone hears your sound change at the same moment. Knob moves you make while recording a take are recorded too (Chapter 17).

    Libraries

    The wall can also open instrument libraries from elsewhere: a GitHub repository (github:owner/repo), a website, or a build served from your own machine. When you play a public library's instrument, your bandmates' browsers load it automatically. A library marked private is never shared: each player adds their own copy, and anyone without it hears the closest built-in stand-in (and is told so).

    Chapter 5Playing

    The computer keyboard

    KeysWhat they do
    A W S E D F T G Y H U J KNotes, like a piano: the middle row is the white keys, the row above the black keys.
    Z XOctave down, octave up.
    C VSofter, harder (velocity).
    SpaceSustain pedal.
    1–6Chord pads, when a key is set (below).

    The on-screen keys play too: click or touch them. With Pads selected, the computer keyboard becomes a 4×4 grid instead (Chapter 7).

    MIDI keyboards and controllers

    Walkthrough: play with a MIDI keyboard
    1. Plug in your keyboard (USB, or a MIDI interface).
    2. Click Enable MIDI on the keys bar and allow it when the browser asks. Once allowed, MIDI switches on by itself next time.
    3. Play. It plays the selected instrument. A controller plugged in later is announced by name.

    To play several instruments from several controllers (a drum pad on channel 10 and a keyboard on channel 1, say), turn on the technical controls and give each instrument a MIDI in channel on its faceplate.

    Web MIDI needs Chrome, Edge or Firefox on a computer. Safari, and browsers on iPhone and iPad, don't offer it.

    The room's key

    Pick a key from Key in the console (major, minor, dorian, mixolydian, phrygian, lydian, pentatonics or blues). Everyone shares it. The notes of the scale light up on the keys, and two things appear:

    • Chord pads above the keys: six chords that fit the key, on 1–6 or by clicking. A strummed instrument such as Acoustic gets all seven, numbered as players count them (hold Shift for the seventh chord). While a section of a song plays, the pad for the bar's chord lights up.
    • Lock to key: every key you press lands on a note of the scale. No wrong notes.

    Rhythm help

    Rhythm help pulls notes you play slightly off the beat part of the way onto it. Your own timing still counts; it just tidies.

    Panic

    Panic stops every note. Use it if something gets stuck.

    Chapter 6Tempo, the metronome and the virtual drummer

    Tempo

    The BPM number is the room's tempo. Click − or +, drag the number, scroll on it, or click it and use the arrow keys. A tempo change starts on a coming beat so everyone switches together. The metronome button beside it clicks the beat for you alone; it never goes into recordings.

    The virtual drummer

    A drummer is a bandmate the room plays for you. It keeps time, fills at the ends of phrases, and follows the song.

    Walkthrough: add a drummer
    1. Open the instrument wall and click the Drummer tile.
    2. Pick a genre and a style: jazz (medium swing, two-feel, up-tempo, ballad, waltz), bossa nova, rock, half-time, funk, boom bap, trap, house, drum & bass, reggae one-drop, afrobeat.
    3. The drummer walks into a corner of its own and comes in on a bar line a couple of seconds later.

    On the drummer's corner:

    • Kit: what it plays on. By default the recorded Studio Kit for acoustic styles and a Machina kit for electronic ones.
    • Intensity: from laid back to driving.
    • Reroll: the same style played by a different drummer.
    • × sends the drummer home.
    • With the technical controls on: Busy (how much it comps), Fills (how often it fills), Feel (in 2, in 4) and listen (it plays up when the band plays more).

    Every browser runs the same drummer from the same settings, so everyone hears the same drums; only the settings travel. A change lands on a bar line a couple of seconds ahead. With a song playing, the drummer follows the sections: it lifts at bridges, builds through solos and ends the song on a big hit.

    Chapter 7Pads: sample pads

    Pads is a sixteen-pad sampler, MPC style. Take it off the wall like any instrument.

    • Playing: click the pads, use MIDI notes 36–51, or the computer keyboard laid out like the grid: Z X C V along the bottom row up to 1 2 3 4 on top.
    • Kits: seventeen to start from, from studio chops and hand drums to orchestral hits, a kalimba and loops chopped a slice per pad (so they play at any tempo). Every sound is free to use or made here.
    • Per pad: trim, tune, gain, and choke groups (a closed hi-hat cutting off an open one).
    Walkthrough: put your own sound on a pad
    1. Select Pads on your desk.
    2. Drop an audio file onto a pad, or pick files, or sample the last bar of your live audio.
    3. Trim and tune it. Your bandmates receive the sound once over the direct links; until it arrives, their browser plays a quick sketch of it.

    Sounds you load are kept in your browser for next time.

    Chapter 8Machina and its sequencer

    Machina is a synthesis drum machine: nine slots (kick, snare, toms, hats, clap and more), each a small synth. No samples.

    The sound

    • Each slot runs a machine (analog circuits, FM percussion, modal physics or raw sources) with pitch, decay, tone, snap, crush, level and pan.
    • The second row of knobs adds body and noise balance, noise colour and decay, drive and velocity response; kick, snare and toms get a pitch sweep; the kick a sub tail; the clap its burst count and spacing.
    • Two LFOs can drive any knob, locked to the room's beat (1/32 to 4 bars, triplets too) or free-running, with five shapes.
    • Notes from C4 up play the perc slot chromatically.

    The step sequencer

    Machina has a 16 to 64-step sequencer with pages. Click steps (trigs) to place hits, set the length, and press play: it starts and stops on the bar.

    Walkthrough: a parameter lock
    1. Hold a trig (or Shift-click it, or turn on LOCK and click it).
    2. While holding, turn a knob.
    3. That one step now plays with that setting: a higher snare, a longer hat.

    The sequencer runs in every browser from the shared pattern and clock, so it sends no notes at all, only your edits. Everyone hears exactly the same hits.

    Chapter 9Playing together

    Walkthrough: invite someone
    1. Click Invite next to the room code.
    2. Read them the four letters, click Copy link (or Share…) and send it, or let them scan the QR code with a phone.
    3. They walk into an empty corner. Up to four players fit in a room.

    Copy a link for listeners gives a link that opens in listening mode: they hear the band but take no corner.

    A bandmate's corner

    The light next to a player's name tells you how their connection is doing (green is good). With the technical controls on, a tag says more: clock leader (the player whose clock everyone follows, normally whoever came first), whether the link is direct or going through the server, and a check that everyone is hearing the same music.

    Chat and reactions

    The speech-bubble tab opens Band chat; a badge counts unread messages. Under the console, the emoji buttons send a quick reaction everyone sees: applause, fire, love, laughter, wow.

    Talk

    Talk sends your voice to the band, like a walkie-talkie:

    • Click it to leave your mic on (it says Talking); click again to turn it off.
    • Hold it while you speak, and it turns off when you let go.

    Your mic only sends while you're actually talking, and the music dips slightly under voices so you can hear each other. A small three-bar mark lights up in the corner of whoever is speaking. Each voice comes from that player's place in the stereo field. Wear headphones, so the band's music doesn't go back out through your mic.

    Leaving

    Leave walks you out. If the clock leader leaves, the next player carries on without a hiccup.

    Chapter 10Timing: how everyone stays together

    You don't need any of this to play: MEGAjam measures and sets everything by itself. This chapter is for the curious, and for when something feels off.

    The shared clock and the lookahead

    Every note is stamped with a moment on a clock the whole room shares, set a little in the future: the lookahead. It's long enough for the note to reach the farthest player, plus a safety margin. Every browser plays the note at that moment, so the band lands together.

    MEGAjam measures how much spare time each player actually has when your notes arrive and adjusts the lookahead continually, rising at once when needed and settling gently. Friends in the same town might need 20 ms; across a continent, 100 ms or more. Bluetooth headphones add a lot (150 ms or more), so wired headphones help.

    The technical controls

    Click the gear button at the foot of the side tabs to show them.

    Look
    The lookahead in milliseconds, with a meter. Drag the slider to set it by hand; AUTO goes back to the measured value.
    Hear myself
    Off (the default): you hear your notes at the same moment the band does, a lookahead after you play them, like playing a pipe organ. On: you hear yourself instantly, and the band hears you a lookahead later. Try both; for slow sounds, on feels nicer.
    Quant
    Quantize: snaps your notes to the next 1/32, 1/16, 1/8 or 1/4 at least a lookahead away, so the delay disappears into the grid.
    Timing (per instrument)
    On the faceplate. Tight (never late: the lookahead plus 10 ms, for drums, bass and plucks), Balanced (the lookahead) or Loose (about 40% of it, and you hear yourself instantly; a far player may get a note a touch late, hidden by a slow attack, for pads and strings). Auto picks from the sound.

    The wiring view

    The wiring tab (technical) lists every link between players: the path (direct or through the server), the round-trip time (RTT), jitter, how late notes arrive, and the lookahead. Players connect to each other directly; a pair that can't (some strict networks) passes messages through the server instead, which adds a little delay, and the corner says so.

    Same music?

    Every couple of seconds, each browser checks that it's playing the same instruments with the same settings as everyone else. If not, the corner shows a warning (it sorts itself out, or rejoining fixes it).

    Chapter 11Recording

    The tape

    Walkthrough: record the jam
    1. Click the red ● button in the console. Everyone sees that you're recording.
    2. Play.
    3. Click it again to stop. Tapes appears.
    4. Open Tapes to play each take back, and Save WAV to keep it.

    The tape records the mix as you heard it: everyone's instruments and live audio, without the metronome. Takes stay only until you close the tab, so save the ones you want.

    Sync take: everyone's true sound

    The tape records what your browser played: real synths come through as their software twins. A sync take records what each player really sounds like, on their own machine, and gathers it all into one folder.

    Walkthrough: a sync take
    1. Click Sync take, then ● Start a sync take. Everyone records.
    2. If you play real hardware, pick which audio input it comes in on.
    3. Play, then ■ Stop the take.
    4. Click Gather everyone's stems. Each player's files come across.
    5. Save the folder downloads a zip: everyone's instruments dry, hardware through its audio input, and every note as MIDI, all lined up from the same moment, ready for any DAW.

    Chapter 12Live audio: mics and guitars

    Instruments in MEGAjam travel as notes and land in time. Real sound from a microphone or a guitar can't beat the network, so it works differently: the band hears it a whole number of bars late, landing on the same chords one loop around.

    That's perfect over a loop or a repeating chord cycle (a solo over the changes, a vocal over a groove) and wrong for tight hits with the band.

    Walkthrough: go live
    1. Open the Live audio tab (the microphone at the side), or Live audio on your shelf.
    2. Pick your input, and how late: 1, 2, 4 or 8 bars.
    3. Click Go live. A meter in your corner shows your level.
    4. If your audio lands a hair early or late, use Nudge.

    While a section of a song loops, 🎙 Lock in my live audio keeps your last full pass as a take that plays from the song for everyone.

    Use headphones. Otherwise the band's music goes back out through your mic.

    Chapter 13Your real synths: My gear

    Bring your hardware. You play the real thing; bandmates who own the same model hear your part on theirs, and everyone else hears its closest software twin.

    Walkthrough: link a synth
    1. Connect your synth by MIDI and click Enable MIDI.
    2. Click My gear on your shelf. MEGAjam lists your MIDI ports and guesses each synth from its name ("is this your OB-6?"). Otherwise, search the list of over 550 units by name, maker, decade or type.
    3. Click Yes, link it (or Link on its row in the list), then Play it. It goes on your desk.
    4. On the faceplate, Twin lists the software instruments that sound most like it, best first, with a match score. Hear twin lets you hear the twin yourself, to compare.
    5. To have MEGAjam play your synth in time with the band, pick it in Plays my synth via and turn Local Control off on the synth. Synth latency fine-tunes it.

    Set up and Check it works

    Each linked synth has a Set up checklist: the steps from its own manual where known (MIDI channel, SysEx on, knobs sending as NRPN or CC, which port), general ones otherwise. Check it works asks the synth who it is and for its current sound, and says what to fix if it doesn't answer.

    I have one too: sharing sounds

    If a bandmate has linked the same model as you, your part plays on their real synth. Your knob moves, program changes and pressure reach it in time with your notes. Where MEGAjam knows how to ask the model for its current sound (77 models so far), your sound is copied across too: their synth loads it on the next bar line, as the current sound, never over a saved program. This needs one extra browser permission (SysEx), asked only when you share sounds.

    Match my sound

    A twin starts as a good guess. Match my sound makes it sound like your patch:

    Walkthrough: match the twin to your synth
    1. Bring your synth's audio into the computer (an audio interface, or the mic input).
    2. At the foot of its faceplate, click Match my sound (it changes to Matching…) and allow the audio input.
    3. Play some single notes, held a moment, across the range.
    4. MEGAjam listens, tries settings on the twin out of earshot, and glides the twin your bandmates hear toward your sound. The status says how alike they are.

    It never touches your synth, runs quietly in the background, and backs off if the jam is under strain.

    Chapter 14MIDI: sequencers, external gear and clock

    Sequencers

    A hardware sequencer or DAW can bring many parts at once over one cable (a Cirklon, Hapax, OXI, or your DAW).

    Walkthrough: add a sequencer
    1. In My gear, click Add a sequencer and start your sequencer playing.
    2. MEGAjam listens and lists every port and channel that has notes.
    3. Say what plays each one: a synth you've linked, or a software instrument (channel 10 is guessed as drums, low lines as bass).
    4. They go on your desk, each listening to its own port and channel.

    MEGAjam sends your sequencer the room's clock, and can run it a lookahead ahead, so its notes land on the room's beat for everyone. Tick MEGAjam plays it for a linked synth and its notes reach it at the moment the band hears them, so you hear the same beat they do.

    External MIDI

    With the technical controls on, + External MIDI on your shelf puts any synth, drum machine or DAW track reachable over MIDI in your corner, including virtual ports (IAC on a Mac, loopMIDI on Windows). Give it a name, an output and a channel, and optionally a twin for the band to hear. Your notes go out to it timed to the moment the band hears your part.

    The MIDI panel

    The MIDI tab (the round DIN-socket icon) does two things:

    • The band's parts on your gear: pick an output and channel for any bandmate's part, and it plays on your own synth or DAW, in time, instead of in the browser.
    • Clock out: sends the room's tempo as MIDI clock, starting on the next bar, so a drum machine or sequencer on your desk runs in step with everyone. early sends it a few milliseconds ahead for slow-to-respond gear.

    Chapter 15The Compose Room: building a song

    The Compose Room turns jams into a song. It's laid out like a DAW: sections across the top, one row per track, time running left to right. Everything in it is shared: every change shows up for the whole band at once.

    The board

    • Sections run across the top: Intro, Verse, Chorus and so on, each sized by its bars, with its chords. Drag a section by its name to move it; its × removes it.
    • Tracks are the rows: one instrument each, with who made it. A block is a track's part in one section: solid with a lock when a take is in, dashed when empty, and striped and pulsing in someone's colour while they're playing it live.
    • A playhead runs across while the song plays.
    • Up front: the song's title, Play the song and the master fader; then the selected section's name, length, chords, Loop it and Jam this section.
    • ▸ Song options holds the take rule, versions, import and export, stems and automation; More holds chord fills, notes, the road-map label and reordering.
    Walkthrough: your first song, start to finish
    1. Click Compose Room in the console.
    2. Select a section, set its length in bars, and type a chord into each bar's box (for example Am, F, C, G).
    3. Add a drum track: + Add track under the tracks, pick a drum instrument, and click in a beat on the empty roll that opens.
    4. Click Loop it to hear the section round and round.
    5. Add a bass track, then Jam it (or Jam this section): you go to the Jam Room with the section looping, a bar counter and the chords above the desk.
    6. Play the bass along. Your last full pass is locked in as a take.
    7. Go back to the Compose Room and click Play the song.

    Sections and the loop strip

    Above the sections runs a loop strip: drag across it to loop any bars (inside a section or across several), drag either end to crop the loop, drag its middle to move it. Changes land on the next bar line without losing your place. A section can also carry a road-map label ("trading 4s", "solo") that the drummer follows.

    Chapter 16Takes, notes and parts

    Locking in takes

    While a section loops, your last full pass on the selected instrument is locked in as a new take in your track, placed where you heard yourself play it. Takes pile up per block. The band picks how a new one lands, under Song options › New takes:

    • take over: the newest take sounds.
    • wait for the owner: the track's owner picks.
    • go to a vote: the band votes.

    Click a block to see its takes: Use this take (or Vote for it). A switch always waits for the top of the next pass, so everyone hears it change together.

    Record every pass

    ⏺ Record every pass on the section bar keeps every full pass you play as a take, without clicking anything: each of your instruments that played, and your live audio. While you record, your old takes stay quiet so nobody hears them over you. Stop, and the newest take sounds. A block keeps its last 32.

    Editing notes

    Click a block (or a track's name) to open its notes in a roll under the section: note names, bar numbers, chords, velocity as brightness.

    • Move: drag a note (it snaps to the grid you pick; hold Alt for none).
    • Resize: drag its end.
    • Add: click (drag for a longer note).
    • Delete: double-click.
    • Quantize: pulls every start toward the grid, at a strength you choose.
    • Clear notes empties the roll; ↶ Undo and ↷ Redo (⌘Z, ⇧⌘Z) step through your changes.
    • Every edit is a new copy of the take; Back to the original returns to it as played.

    Writing parts from scratch

    Walkthrough: write a part with step entry
    1. Under a section's chords, pick ✎ Write a part for… (a track already in the song, or a new one for an instrument on your desk), or click an empty block.
    2. An empty roll opens. Click notes in, or turn on 🎹 Step entry.
    3. Pick a note length, then play a note or chord on a MIDI keyboard or the computer keys. It lands at the cursor, which moves on when you let go.
    4. Rest ▶ skips a step; click a bar number to jump there.

    A written part is part of the song, so every browser has it in full before it plays.

    Tracks

    A track's settings sit above its takes: rename it, change its instrument or patch (the whole track follows, as in a DAW), Jam it (its instrument goes on your desk, and what you lock goes into this track), or Delete track. A track also deletes from the 🗑 on its row, or Delete on its header. It goes for everyone, and a bar offers Undo for twelve seconds.

    Chapter 17Mixing, automation, versions and export

    The mix

    • Each track has a fader on its row, and Volume (−∞ to +6 dB) and Pan in its settings. You hear a move as you drag; when you let go it's saved for everyone. Double-click puts either back to the middle.
    • M mutes a track for everyone; S solos it for you alone.
    • The master fader beside Play the song sets the whole song's level.
    The faders on the players' corners are different: they set how loud each live player is to you, and change nothing for anyone else.

    Automatic mastering

    Everything the room plays goes through a mastering console: tracks fold into eight stems (drums, bass, chords, pads, melody, solo, hits, texture), each ridden to its own level, then glued, balanced and limited to a loudness that suits the song's style. It's on from the start. ▸ Mastering beside the master fader opens it: on or off, style, auto levels, loudness, ceiling, glue, a level per stem, and meters.

    Automation

    Knob moves (and pitch bends) are always recorded with a take and play back with it, for everyone. Knobs go back to the track's settings when the block ends. A block whose take moves knobs shows a small ◇.

    Walkthrough: draw automation
    1. Song options › Show automation. Each track gets a ◇.
    2. Click a track's ◇ to open a lane per knob: the track's setting dashed, the moves as a line.
    3. Click to add a point, drag to move it (snaps to 1/16; Alt for none), double-click to remove it.
    4. + automate a knob… draws any other knob from scratch; × clears a knob.

    Versions

    Song options › Versions › Save this version… keeps the current choice of takes under a name ("the one with Matt's solo"). Go back to it any time.

    Starting from a preset or a sketch

    • ♪ Song presets… starts a song from a form, chord changes and a basic band (drums, bass, chords): five each in twenty genres.
    • Import a sketch… takes a MIDI file (tracks become parts, markers become sections, tempo and key come along) or a song from SynthLand's Tracker.
    • Save MIDI and Save song export the song as a MIDI file or a MEGAjam song file.

    Exporting stems

    Walkthrough: export stems for your DAW
    1. Song options › Export stems…
    2. Choose Download a .zip, or (Chrome and Edge) Save into a folder…. Just my tracks exports only yours.
    3. You get every track as a 24-bit WAV from bar 1 to the end, all the same length, after each track's fader and pan; plus the mix, the song as MIDI, a note with the tempo and key, and an Ableton Live set (Live 11 or later) with every stem in place.
    4. Drop them into any DAW at the song's tempo and they line up.

    Band folder: paste your band's Google Drive or Dropbox folder link, and everyone sees it. Each player can then Save into a folder… their synced copy of it, so everyone's stems land together.

    Chapter 18Phones, tablets and AirPlay

    MEGAjam works on phones and tablets: the studio fits the screen, and the on-screen keys and pads take touch. A few limits:

    • No MIDI on iPhone and iPad (browsers there don't offer it).
    • Tap once when asked: phones keep sound off until you touch the page.

    In Safari on iPhone and iPad, AirPlay in the console sends the room's sound to a speaker. It adds about two seconds of delay, and says so first: fine for listening, too late to play along. If Safari can't send it, use AirPlay from Control Center.

    Chapter 20Troubleshooting

    I can't hear anything.
    Tap or click anywhere on the page (browsers keep sound off until you do). Check that the player's corner isn't muted (M) or turned down, that an instrument is selected on your desk, and your computer's volume.
    My MIDI keyboard does nothing.
    Use Chrome, Edge or Firefox on a computer, and click Enable MIDI. If you set a MIDI in channel on the instrument (technical controls), make sure your keyboard sends on it.
    Everything feels a bit late.
    That's the lookahead: the price of everyone landing together. Check you aren't on Bluetooth headphones (they add a lot); wired is better. Turn on Hear myself to hear your own notes instantly. For slow sounds, set the instrument's Timing to Loose.
    A friend's corner says the link goes through the server.
    Their network won't allow a direct link, so messages pass through MEGAjam's server. It works, just with a little more delay.
    A corner says it's out of sync.
    It usually sorts itself out within a few seconds. If not, that player can leave and walk back in.
    A note is stuck.
    Click Panic.
    The band hears an echo of themselves when I talk.
    Wear headphones, so the music doesn't go back out through your mic.
    Someone left, but their corner is still there.
    If they closed the tab without leaving, their corner usually goes within a few seconds, as soon as their link closes.
    Does MEGAjam send anything about my jams?
    Yes: anonymous numbers that show how well jams work, so we can fix what doesn't. They cover how long joining took, whether links were direct or through the server, round-trip times, the lookahead, clock error, late notes, stalls, your kind of device and browser, which instruments you used, and any errors the page hit. MEGAjam never sends names, chat, the notes you play, audio, or your room code (only a scrambled version of it, so one jam's numbers can be grouped). The stats server also notes your country, but not your IP address, and keeps reports for 90 days. To turn it off in this browser, open MEGAjam once with ?stats=0 at the end of the address (?stats=1 turns it back on).

    Chapter 21Glossary

    Block
    A track's part in one section of the song.
    Clock leader
    The player whose clock the room follows; normally whoever came first.
    Corner
    Your place in the studio, holding your instruments.
    Lock in
    Keep a pass you played over a looping section as a take in the song.
    Lockstep
    Every browser runs everyone's instruments and plays every note at the same moment on a shared clock. Only the notes travel.
    Lookahead
    How far in the future notes are scheduled, so they reach everyone in time.
    Patch
    A saved setting of an instrument's knobs: a starting sound.
    Section
    A part of the song (Verse, Chorus…), with a length in bars and chords.
    Stem
    One track (or group of tracks) rendered to its own audio file.
    Take
    One recorded or written version of a block.
    Track
    One instrument in the song, a row on the board.
    Twin
    The software instrument that stands in for a real synth, for bandmates who don't own one.
